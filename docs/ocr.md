# Reading receipts

A photographed receipt or a PDF invoice becomes a filled-in transaction form without leaving the
device. The pipeline is Tesseract and pdf.js compiled to WebAssembly, a layout pass, and a parser
that knows what an Ontario receipt looks like. Nothing is uploaded to read it, and there is no API
key.

## The pipeline

| Step         | Where                                                         | What it does                                                                                                              |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Prepare      | [src/lib/ocr/preprocess.ts](../src/lib/ocr/preprocess.ts)     | Scales the photo into the range Tesseract reads best, greys it and stretches contrast between the 2nd and 98th percentile |
| Recognise    | [src/lib/ocr/engine.ts](../src/lib/ocr/engine.ts)             | Runs the WASM worker, returns every word with its box and confidence                                                      |
| Rebuild rows | [src/lib/ocr/layout.ts](../src/lib/ocr/layout.ts)             | Groups words by vertical centre, sorts each row left to right                                                             |
| Parse        | [src/lib/ocr/parseReceipt.ts](../src/lib/ocr/parseReceipt.ts) | Vendor, date, totals, line items, category, confidence                                                                    |

A PDF takes one of two routes ([pdf.ts](../src/lib/ocr/pdf.ts)). If it carries a text layer — which
every invoice generated by software does — the text is read straight out of the file with its
coordinates, no OCR and no loss: a `847` fragment and a `.50` fragment sitting flush against each
other are rejoined into `847.50`, and a label at x=72 is paired with its amount at x=430. If the
text layer is thin, under 40 characters, the file is a scan; its pages are rendered to canvas at up
to 1600px wide and handed to Tesseract. Either way at most three pages are read, stacked into one
page of lines.

The rebuild step is not optional. A receipt has a label column and a price column with a wide gap
between them, and Tesseract reads that as two separate blocks: every label, then every price. Row
reconstruction from word boxes puts `Kale Salad` back next to `14.00` no matter how the engine
segmented the page.

## What the parser decides

- **Money** ([money.ts](../src/lib/ocr/money.ts)) is a number with exactly two decimals. Digit
  lookalikes are repaired only inside tokens that already look like money, so `5.5O` becomes `5.50`
  while `C0FFEE` is left alone.
- **Totals** ([totals.ts](../src/lib/ocr/totals.ts)) are labelled first and reconciled second.
  A line is classified as subtotal, tip, tax or total in that order, so `SUBTOTAL` and `TOTAL TAX`
  never land in the wrong bucket. Whichever of the three is missing is derived from the other two.
- **Tax is never invented.** No tax label on the receipt means `isTaxed: false` and subtotal equals
  total, which is what a zero-rated grocery run should produce. A tax label whose amount was
  unreadable is the one case where the rate is applied to the subtotal, and that reading is marked
  unreconciled so its confidence drops.
- **Dates** ([dates.ts](../src/lib/ocr/dates.ts)) accept ISO, numeric and month-name forms. An
  ambiguous `03/04/2026` is read month-first unless that lands in the future, which resolves most
  Canadian receipts correctly. A date more than ten years old or more than two days ahead is
  rejected rather than trusted.
- **Line items** ([items.ts](../src/lib/ocr/items.ts)) are the rows above the totals block that end
  in a price. They are always returned, but the form only uses them as its rows when they add up to
  the parsed subtotal — `itemsCoverSubtotal`. Otherwise the form writes one row for the subtotal and
  the items stay in the document's stored reading.
- **The category** ([classify.ts](../src/lib/ocr/classify.ts)) is scored from the keyword registry
  in [classify.registry.ts](../src/lib/ocr/classify.registry.ts): a vendor-name hit weighs more than
  a body hit, matching is whole-word, and a weak score returns nothing rather than a guess. Applying
  a category also applies that category's claimable percentage, which is why a read restaurant bill
  arrives at 50%.

Confidence for a text-layer PDF starts at 96 rather than a measured score, because the characters
were read, not guessed. Confidence is otherwise the engine's own score multiplied by how many structural checks passed — totals
reconcile, a date was found, a vendor was found, items add up. Below `0.6` the form says so and the
document is stored as `needs_review`; at or above it the document is stored as `parsed`. The whole
reading, including the raw text, is written to `rawOcrJson` when the document is captured, so a
later pass can re-parse without re-running OCR.

## Engine assets

The Tesseract worker, its WASM core, the English model, the pdf.js worker and the pdf.js standard
fonts are served from `public/ocr/`, which is gitignored and populated by
[scripts/sync-ocr-assets.js](../scripts/sync-ocr-assets.js) on `postinstall` or `npm run ocr:assets`.
The standard fonts matter for text extraction, not just rendering: without them pdf.js cannot map
glyphs in a PDF that uses Helvetica or Courier without embedding it. Self-hosting them keeps the CDN out of the loop, which is what lets OCR work
offline; the service worker caches `/ocr/` on first use and Tesseract caches the model in
IndexedDB. The paths are declared in [engine.ts](../src/lib/ocr/engine.ts) and
[pdf.ts](../src/lib/ocr/pdf.ts) and mirrored in the sync script — change one and change the other.

The worker is started on the first read and terminated after 45 seconds idle, because an idle
Tesseract worker holds on to its heap.

## Limits

PDFs, and images the browser can decode; a HEIC photo is read only where the browser decodes it.
Anything else is stored as a reference with `ocrStatus: "pending"` and the form stays blank. Three
pages per document. English only. Handwriting is not read.
